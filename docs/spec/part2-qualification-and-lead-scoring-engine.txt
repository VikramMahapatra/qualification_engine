
Part 2 — Qualification &amp; Lead Scoring Engine
1. Purpose
The Qualification &amp; Lead Scoring Engine evaluates a completed customer conversation and determines:
Whether the lead is Qualified or Not Qualified
If Qualified, the Lead Score between 1 and 100
If Qualified, the Lead Temperature:
Cold
Warm
Hot
Very Hot
The engine uses the Qualification Template associated with the chatbot agent, inbound calling agent, outbound calling agent, or campaign.
The same engine must work across different industries, business models, customer types, and conversation channels.

2. Engine Input
The engine uses two primary inputs:
A. Conversation Data
The completed conversation or call transcript.
The transcript is the source of evidence about what the customer actually said or communicated.
B. Qualification Template
The Qualification Template provides the business rules configured by the client.
The engine uses:
Campaign / Agent Objective
Qualified Lead requirements
Business / Qualification Attributes
Positive Signals
Disqualification criteria
Temperature ranges
The engine must not depend on an existing lead score, existing outcome, or sentiment result.

3. Overall Engine Flow
The engine follows the below business flow:
Conversation / Transcript
↓
Understand Customer Conversation
↓
Identify Relevant Evidence
↓
Check Disqualification
↓
Evaluate Qualification Requirements
↓
Determine Qualified / Not Qualified
↓
If Not Qualified
Outcome = Negative
Processing ends.
If Qualified
↓
Evaluate Interest &amp; Buying Intent
↓
Calculate Lead Score (1–100)
↓
Apply Temperature Range
↓
Assign Cold / Warm / Hot / Very Hot
↓
Final Lead Result

The engine should evaluate the meaning and context of the conversation rather than relying only on exact words.
Example
Configured Positive Signal:

Customer says:
“How much would this cost?”
This should be recognized as a Price Enquiry.
The customer does not need to use the exact phrase “price enquiry.”

5. Campaign / Agent Objective
The Campaign / Agent Objective provides the overall context for the evaluation.
Example:
“Identify customers who are genuinely interested in purchasing our products and are likely to take the next step.”
The engine uses this objective to understand the overall purpose of the conversation evaluation.
The objective provides context; the actual qualification decision is made using the configured qualification and disqualification criteria.

6. Disqualification Evaluation
The engine first checks whether the conversation clearly establishes any configured disqualification condition.
Examples:
Explicitly not interested
No requirement
Wrong customer / profile
Requirement outside our offering
Outside service area
Not the intended customer
Requirement does not match our offering
Requests no further contact
Custom disqualification
If a disqualification condition is clearly established
The result is:
Qualification = Not Qualified
Outcome = Negative
The lead does not proceed to temperature classification.
Important
A negative signal must be supported by the actual conversation.
The engine should not assume that a customer is disqualified merely because a particular piece of information was not mentioned.

7. Qualification Evaluation
If no configured disqualification condition is established, the engine evaluates the Qualified Lead requirements.
The engine supports three qualification methods configured in the Qualification Template.

7.1 Essential + Supporting
When Essential + Supporting is selected:
Every Essential requirement must be satisfied.
Supporting requirements are additional evidence.
Supporting requirements are not mandatory for qualification.
Example

Importance

Essential

If the conversation establishes:
✓ Genuine need / requirement✓ Relevant use case✓ Specific requirement
but does not establish Future requirement:
Result = Qualified
because all Essential requirements have been satisfied.
Supporting requirements may subsequently contribute to the strength of the lead.

7.2 Any Selected Requirement
When Any selected requirement is selected:
The lead can be Qualified when at least one selected qualification requirement is clearly established in the conversation, provided that no disqualification condition applies.
Example

Customer says:
“Yes, I’m interested in your product.”
Result:

because one selected qualification requirement has been satisfied.

7.3 All Selected Requirements
When All selected requirements is selected:
Every selected qualification requirement must be established in the conversation.
Example

✓ Genuine need✓ Relevant use case✗ Purchase intention not established
Result:
Not Qualified

8. Business / Qualification Attributes
The engine evaluates Business / Qualification Attributes configured by the client.
Only attributes marked:
Qualification Relevant = Yes
are used as part of qualification.
Attributes marked:
No — Information only
are captured as useful customer information but do not affect qualification.

8.1 Attribute Evaluation
The engine identifies the customer’s value for a configured attribute from the conversation and compares it with the qualification requirement configured by the client.
Example
Attribute:

Data Type:
Amount / Currency
Qualification Relevant:
Yes
Importance:
Essential
Relevant Range:
₹1,00,000 – ₹5,00,000
Customer says:
“We have a budget of around three lakh.”
The engine identifies:
Budget = approximately ₹3,00,000
The value falls within the configured range.
Therefore:
Budget requirement = Satisfied

9. Insufficient Evidence
The engine must distinguish between:
Not Qualified
The conversation provides evidence that the lead does not satisfy the qualification requirements.
Qualified
The conversation provides sufficient evidence that the qualification requirements are satisfied.
Insufficient Evidence
The conversation does not contain enough information to establish qualification.
When insufficient evidence is encountered, the engine follows the client’s configured setting under Disqualification.
Default Behaviour
Do not automatically qualify
Therefore:
Insufficient evidence → Not Qualified
This prevents the platform from treating an unclear or incomplete conversation as a positive lead.
If the client has enabled:
Allow overall conversation assessment
the engine may use the overall conversation to determine whether the customer nevertheless demonstrates sufficient evidence of qualification.

10. Qualification Result
After evaluating the qualification requirements, the engine produces one of two primary results:
Qualified
The conversation satisfies the configured qualification conditions.
Not Qualified
The conversation does not satisfy the configured qualification conditions, or a configured disqualification condition has been established.

12. Lead Score
The Lead Score represents the strength of the qualified lead.
The score is always between:
1 and 100
The score is generated only after the lead has been determined to be Qualified.
A Not Qualified lead does not require a lead temperature.

13. How the Score Is Determined
The engine evaluates the strength of the evidence found in the conversation.
The primary areas considered are:
Requirement
How clearly has the customer demonstrated a genuine requirement?
Interest
How strongly and actively is the customer showing interest?
Commercial Intent
Has the customer moved into commercial discussions such as:
Price
Budget
Quantity
Package
Payment terms
Quotation
Proposal
Next-Step Intent
Has the customer indicated a desire to take a next step such as:
Receiving information
Callback
Meeting
Demo
Visit
Quotation
Purchase
Booking
Another configured next step
Timing
How close is the customer to taking action?
For example:
Future
Undefined
Defined period
Near-term
Immediate
The engine combines the evidence from these areas and produces the final Lead Score between 1 and 100.

14. Score Calculation Principle
The client does not enter numerical weights for individual qualification requirements or positive signals.
The platform maintains the underlying scoring model centrally.
This means the client configures business meaning, while the platform determines how that evidence translates into a consistent score.
For example:
A customer who only expresses general interest should receive a lower score than a customer who:
Has a specific requirement
Discusses price
Provides quantity
Has a defined timeline
Requests a quotation
Indicates an intention to purchase
The stronger the demonstrated intent, the higher the resulting score.

15. Score and Temperature Are Different
The Lead Score and Lead Temperature are separate concepts.
Lead Score
A numerical measurement:
1–100
It provides a more granular representation of lead strength.
Lead Temperature
A category based on the score:
Cold / Warm / Hot / Very Hot
Temperature is therefore assigned after the score has been calculated.

21. Core Business Rules
The following rules define the Qualification &amp; Lead Scoring Engine:
The engine evaluates the conversation against the attached Qualification Template.
Qualification and lead scoring are separate decisions.
Disqualification conditions are evaluated before final qualification.
A clearly established disqualification results in Not Qualified.
Qualification requirements are evaluated according to the configured qualification method.
Essential requirements must be satisfied when Essential + Supporting is selected.
At least one requirement must be satisfied when Any Selected Requirement is selected.
Every selected requirement must be satisfied when All Selected Requirements is selected.
Only Qualification-Relevant Business Attributes affect qualification.
Information-only attributes do not affect qualification.
Positive Signals provide evidence of customer interest and buying intent.
Only Qualified leads proceed to lead scoring and temperature assignment.
The Lead Score is always between 1 and 100.
The platform controls the underlying scoring model; clients configure business criteria rather than numerical weights.
Lead Temperature is derived from the Lead Score.
Temperature ranges are configurable by the client.
Temperature ranges must cover the complete 1–100 range without gaps or overlaps.
Insufficient evidence must not automatically result in a positive qualification under the default configuration.
The engine evaluates the meaning and context of customer statements rather than relying only on exact words.
The same conversation evaluated against the same Qualification Template should produce a consistent qualification and scoring result.
